Evidence.

Numbers on brand, AI search, and agents that you can cite in a presentation without having to take them back later. Every entry names the primary source, the sample, the date, and the limit of what it proves. Ready to copy, with attribution.

Most numbers in this field circulate without a source, with the wrong denominator, or a year too late. I need them for my own writing, so I check them against the primary source. The result is here, including where it contradicts my own position.

A number is not the same as its interpretation. So every entry states what it explicitly does not prove. If you read a number differently or find an error, write to me and I will correct it and note the date.

Topic

Verified study

97 percent of all llms.txt files received no request at all in May 2026.

What the number does not say: It measures requests in server logs, not effect. The file remains useful for coding and browser agents. What is refuted is the claim that AI search reads llms.txt for its recommendations.

Ahrefs, server-log analysis across 137,210 domains · May 2026 · Source

Permalink

Verified study

An analysis across 300,000 domains found no measurable effect of llms.txt on citations in AI answers.

What the number does not say: No effect in this measurement does not mean the file is useless. It is the second independent study with the same result, which makes it the sturdier half of the finding above.

SE Ranking, analysis across 300,000 domains · 2026 · Source

Permalink

Vendor documentation

Google explicitly states that llms.txt, special markup, and custom chunking are not needed for AI search.

What the statement does not say: It applies to Google, not to other systems, and it concerns visibility in search, not whether agents can use a page. From Google’s perspective, optimizing for AI search is still SEO.

Google Search Central, official guide · as of July 2026 · Source

Permalink

Single test

Adding JSON-LD to 1,885 pages showed no measurable effect on AI citations against 4,000 control pages.

What the test does not say: It measures citations in ChatGPT and Google AI Mode, not whether structured data is read at all. For rich results in classic search the markup remains uncontested. One test is not proof of no effect.

Ahrefs, 1,885 pages against 4,000 control pages · 2026 · Source

Permalink

Verified study

Brand mentions correlate with AI visibility far more strongly than backlinks.

What the number does not say: Correlation is not causation. Large brands are mentioned more often and cited more often without one causing the other. What holds is the ranking: what third parties write weighs more than your own technique.

Ahrefs, analysis across 75,000 brands · 2026 · Source

Permalink

Verified study

Language models recommend widely varying brands when asked the identical question.

What follows from it: Anyone measuring AI visibility with a single run is mostly measuring noise. A measurement becomes meaningful only across several runs, several systems, and repeated points in time. That applies to the numbers visibility tools report, too.

SparkToro, research on the consistency of AI recommendations · 2026 · Source

Permalink

Verified study

On 30.6 percent of one million home pages surveyed, buttons had no accessible name.

What the number does not say: It was made as an accessibility survey, not an agent test. The connection is direct nonetheless: a button without a name has no label in the accessibility tree, and agents working from that tree cannot name it. Home pages were measured, not entire websites.

WebAIM Million, one million home pages · 2026 · Source

Permalink

Preliminary, prototype

In a prototype, an agent succeeded on 89.3 percent of tasks on the machine-legible version against 49.3 percent on the original.

What the experiment does not say: What varied was machine clarity, not accessibility, and it is two versions of a single page in a lab setting. The connection is plausible but not transferable to arbitrary websites. It is the best available indication, not a proof.

Designing Agent-Ready Websites, arXiv 2607.12056 · July 2026 · Source

Permalink

Usually miscited

The widely cited drop in agent success from 78 to 42 percent comes from a study that changed no website at all.

What actually varied: how the agent operates, not the accessibility of the pages. The study took the agent’s mouse away, so to speak. As evidence that accessible websites help agents it does not hold, although it is cited for exactly that everywhere. A clean comparison of accessible against inaccessible is still missing.

A11y-CUA, CHI 2026, arXiv 2602.09310 · 2026 · Source

Permalink

Three independent tests

On direct fetch by AI systems, JavaScript is generally not executed.

What the finding does not say: It holds for the direct fetch, not for content that reaches an answer through the Google index. A telling side finding in the third test: with a phone number hidden behind JavaScript, some vendors returned it and others did not. That is a vendor decision, not a technical limit.

searchviu (2025), Resoneo (2026) and Search Engine World (2026) · Source

Permalink

Vendor documentation

Lighthouse has audited machine readability for agents in its default configuration since version 13.3.0.

What the score does not say: It measures agent readiness, explicitly not visibility in Google search. A good value is no ranking promise. What is notable is the direction: machine readability turns from a claim into a measured property.

Google, Lighthouse category Agentic Browsing · since May 2026 · Source

Permalink

Vendor documentation

Google’s own agent reads pages through the accessibility tree.

What does not follow: that every agent works this way. Among the common tools some read the tree, others work primarily from the DOM, others combine both. The generalization that all browser agents read the accessibility tree is false and circulates anyway.

Google, documentation on its own agent · 2026 · Source

Permalink

State of standardization

A common standard for how websites permit or refuse AI use of their content still does not exist.

What that means in practice: Anyone steering AI crawlers today does so through robots.txt and vendor-specific tokens that can change at any time. The IETF working group AIPREF is building a shared vocabulary; a single vendor push toward one expired without becoming binding.

IETF, working group AIPREF · ongoing · Source

Permalink

Court decision

Air Canada is liable for the refund its chatbot promised; the tribunal rejected the defence that the bot was a separate legal entity.

What the case is not: a German or European ruling. It comes from British Columbia and binds no one here. As a precedent for the underlying question it is still the most cited case: what the agent promises, the brand promises.

British Columbia Civil Resolution Tribunal, Moffatt v. Air Canada · February 2024 · Source

Permalink

Documented incident

Cursor’s support agent invented a company policy that never existed, appearing under a human name.

What the case shows: The damage came not from a wrong answer alone but from the fact that it sounded like a binding company rule. Cancellations and a public apology followed. A single incident, but with the same pattern as the others: the agent speaks as the brand.

The Register · April 2025 · Source

Permalink

Market observation

Buying directly inside the chat was rolled back half a year after launch; fewer than thirty merchants had it live.

What follows and what does not: The thesis that commerce moves wholesale into the chat is refuted for now. What is not refuted is the shift in discovery: found at the agent, bought at the brand. In parallel, several vendors are building protocols for agentic purchase.

Forrester, analysis of the instant checkout rollback · 2026 · Source

Permalink

Case report

Walmart has an AI negotiate autonomously with more than 2,000 suppliers: three percent average savings, 68 percent closing rate.

What the numbers are: figures from the reported programme, not an independent audit. The third number is the notable one: three quarters of suppliers said they preferred negotiating with the system over a person. That is mandate in procurement, quantified and in production.

IEEE Spectrum, report on the negotiation system · Source

Permalink

Survey

44 percent of US online shoppers begin their journey wholly or partly inside a language model.

What the number does not say: It rests on self-reporting and covers the US, not the German-speaking market. A journey begun is also not a purchase. As an order of magnitude for the shift in discovery it is usable; as a revenue statement it is not.

Bain & Company, US consumer survey · 2026 · Source

Permalink

Survey

Consumers want AI help while shopping but mostly do not want AI to make the purchase decision.

Why it matters: It is the counterweight to the forecasts about autonomously buying agents. Stated preference and later behaviour do diverge regularly, and surveys are especially prone to it here because the experience is missing.

Gartner, consumer survey · May 2026 · Source

Permalink

Forecast

Gartner expects machine customers to account for 15 to 20 percent of revenue in relevant categories by 2030.

What this is: a forecast, not a measurement. It belongs in a presentation only with that word in front of it. Analyst forecasts for new categories are often wrong, usually in the timing rather than the direction.

Gartner, machine customers · Source

Permalink

Legal status, in motion

The EU AI Act transparency duties for chatbots and AI-generated content apply provisionally from 2 August 2026; the high-risk duties were postponed to December 2027 and August 2028.

Why provisionally is in that sentence: The deadlines have been moved repeatedly, most recently by the Digital Omnibus in May 2026. Anyone planning around these dates should re-check them before use. The direction is stable, the calendar is not.

EU AI Act, Article 50, and the Digital Omnibus (May 2026) · as of August 2026 · Source

Permalink

Vendor figures

The large user numbers circulating for AI search systems come mostly from the vendors themselves.

How to handle them: Figures quoted include around 900 million weekly active users for ChatGPT and roughly one billion for Google AI Mode, plus individual retailer claims about fast-growing referral traffic. None of them is independently audited. They work as an order of magnitude, not as evidence, and belong in a sentence that names the source.

How this collection is built

An entry is added only after I have checked it against the primary source for a text of my own. No entry rests on a summary of a summary. Where only a report about a study was available to me, the report is named as the source, not the study.

The limit belongs to the number. The most common error in this field is not the wrong number but the right number carrying a claim that reaches further than the evidence. That is why every entry has two parts, and the second one matters more.

This page ages. Every entry carries its date, the page carries its state. Numbers that are superseded get replaced, not quietly deleted.
